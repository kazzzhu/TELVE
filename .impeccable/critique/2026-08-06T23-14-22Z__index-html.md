## Overall Impression

Your instinct was right — the service cards are competent but interchangeable. The palette and typography carry the "industrial workshop" identity everywhere else on the site (hero, nav, hazard stripe), but the one component your buyers actually study to judge your capability doesn't carry that identity itself — it's a clean, safe, generic feature-card template. The biggest opportunity: make the hazard-stripe motif (already established as TELVE's visual signature in the hero) show up structurally inside this component, not just decoratively at the top of the page.

## What's Working

- **Graceful image degradation**: `onerror="this.remove()"` + a monospace `.ph` placeholder is reused consistently everywhere a client photo might be missing — a genuinely good real-world engineering choice for a client who supplies photos on their own schedule.
- **Two-audience neutrality**: nothing in the credibility band (client logos, stats strip) visually favors SIDOR/Corpoelec over a homeowner — matches your stated principle of serving both audiences equally.
- **Vocabulary match**: the service descriptions use real industry terms (Megger, Ducter, Hi-Pot, Baker) without dumbing them down — reads as written by someone who actually does this work, not marketing copy.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] The service card has no structural signature tying it to TELVE.**
Why it matters: this is exactly the component you flagged as "básico," and it's also the page an institutional buyer studies most closely when comparing vendors.
Fix: bring the hazard-stripe motif into the card itself — e.g. a thin diagonal-stripe seam between the photo and the text block — instead of confining it to the hero banner. Make it recognizably TELVE even with the color turned off.
Suggested command: `/impeccable bolder` (targeting `.service`)

**[P1] Three different "card" accent treatments coexist with no stated reason.**
Why it matters: `.card` gets a 3px red top border, `.card--plain` gets a small red rule-mark, `.service` gets no accent at all — an inconsistency that reads as unfinished rather than deliberate.
Fix: give `.service` the same red top-accent the other cards use, or document in DESIGN.md exactly why it's the exception.
Suggested command: `/impeccable polish`

**[P2] In-page navigation breaks the browser Back button.**
Why it matters: `goTo()` (js/script.js) only calls `history.replaceState`, never `pushState`, so a visitor who clicks Servicios → Nosotros can't press Back to return to Servicios.
Fix: use `pushState` for user-initiated nav clicks; keep `replaceState` only for the initial hash-sync on page load.
Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

**[P3] Red is overused as a decorative bullet color, diluting your own "Signal Red Rule."**
Why it matters: `.service__list li::before` colors every bullet mark red — about 26 non-actionable red marks across the 4 service cards — which works against DESIGN.md's own rule that red should mean "action," making the real accents (folio numeral, tags) less special.
Fix: recolor bullets to teal or muted gray.
Suggested command: `/impeccable typeset`

## Persona Red Flags

**Sam (accessibility-dependent)**: Nav items are `<span data-nav="...">` with only a `click` listener — no `tabindex`, no `role`, no keyboard handler. A keyboard-only or screen-reader visitor cannot Tab to or activate Inicio/Servicios/Nosotros/Contacto at all. This is a real barrier, not a nice-to-have.

**Jordan (first-timer, but really: anyone with JS disabled or slow to load)**: Servicios/Nosotros/Contacto are marked `hidden` in the raw HTML and only revealed by JS. There's no `<noscript>` fallback — if JS fails, a visitor only ever sees Inicio.

**Casey (mobile)**: Below 900px the service cards collapse to a single column with 7 stacked bullets each, repeated 4 times in one continuous scroll — the exact page under review becomes a long, dense wall on the device most visitors will actually use.

## Minor Observations

- `.step__num` (the 01/02/03 in "El proceso") renders at very low contrast (~1.3:1) — fine as a pure decorative mark, but redundant screen-reader noise since `.step__title` already says the same thing in words.
- The service folio numeral and the process-step numeral use the same "big Anton number" pattern but different colors (red vs. light gray) for conceptually the same idea — worth a second look to see if that's intentional.
- Tag chips (outlined) and brand chips (solid) look related but are styled differently between service #4 (brands) and services #1-3 (tags) — confirm that's an intentional category distinction.
